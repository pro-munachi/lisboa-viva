# City appearance and transport camera review

These requests extend ROAM's existing Lisbon pilot. Lagos Life is a reference for readable, playful map interactions; all models, signs and category glyphs here are original. The map continues to use the real geographic bundle rather than rearranged venue clusters.

## Video reference review — 9 October 2026

Reviewed the user-supplied [Lagos Life riding clip](https://x.com/TrenchesDiary/status/2108328907598659818?s=20) and [Danfo Simulator trailer](https://x.com/Developerjoe123/status/2108474630616875155?s=20) in the browser. These observations describe visible footage, not verified game mechanics or ROAM features already implemented.

- **Lagos Life:** a close camera follows a motorbike along connected streets. Individual shop signs and colourful merchandise, pedestrians in different clothes, patterned pavements, streetlights, trees, marked lanes and yellow/black raised kerbs make the street legible at player height. The compact rounded HUD leaves the scenery visible.
- **Danfo:** varied neighbourhoods and roof forms, dense traffic, a bus parking area, overhead utility wires, police checkpoints with cones and barriers, waterfront bridges and a distant skyline give the city scale. Sunset colour, reflective water, illuminated windows and bright night signage give different scenes distinct moods. The driving sequence displays a route, passenger count, minimap and dialogue; the trailer does not by itself establish which scenery objects are clickable or the collision rules.
- **Direction for ROAM:** carry colour into the world through façades, storefronts, clothing, vehicle details, planting and lighting. Make road edges, pavements and destinations recognisable from a following camera. Give transport and NPCs purposeful movement and readable feedback. Preserve ROAM's rounded emoji controls and Lisbon geography while designing original local scenery and interactions.

The follow-up navigation, vehicles, infrastructure and scenery actions are integrated into the live scene. They extend the merged PR #9 snapshot; the review record below distinguishes the current checks from earlier increments.

### Street simulation follow-up

1. **Make the city readable at walking height.** More saturated façades, terracotta roofs, blue water, dark asphalt and cream/dark calçada paving carry colour into the world. Night windows have stable, varied warm light. Mapped streets receive original crossings, stop stripes, traffic lights, give-way signs, bus shelters, recycling bins, bicycle racks, fountains and street-name plates. Signals share the vehicle simulation's junctions and clock. These are illustrative placements on mapped roads, not a survey of present-day Lisbon infrastructure.
2. **Give traffic recognisable vehicles and destinations.** Original sedans, hatchbacks, black/green taxis, yellow city/regional buses and trams replace the simple traffic boxes. Nearby traffic traverses a connected road network, turns at junctions, slows for signals and pedestrians, leaves the active area and later enters on a different road. Buses pause at stops. Bus/taxi travel sequences reuse these models with animated wheels and boarding doors. One-way data is respected where retained by the geographic snapshot; it does not preserve every source road-direction tag.
3. **Walk around objects with a following camera.** Walk mode supports canvas-focused WASD/arrow keys and clicked destinations. Continuous collision sweeps and wall sliding cover source building footprints, trunks, reserved furniture, water, nearby NPCs, vehicles and real-player capsules. Walking routes prefer the mapped pedestrian network. Close cameras choose an exterior angle and avoid building envelopes; the avatar's actual movement drives the follow camera. Scenic walkers follow connected safe pavement routes, pause at destinations and yield with their companions near players. Remote-player collision is client-side; it is not server-authoritative multiplayer physics.
4. **Keep roads and buildings stable during rotation.** Detailed roads and surfaces suppress their matching coarse source features, rather than leaving two coplanar versions. Roads use bounded bevel joins and terrain samples; exact duplicate building footprints are removed while separate relation wings survive. The distant overview masks whole simplified buildings near detailed chunks. Camera clipping adapts to the distance, and detail ownership restores the coarse layer after nearby chunks leave view.
5. **Make small scenery discoveries playable.** Click an object or open **View options → Things around you**. Rounded emoji action cards offer sitting/relaxing at benches, identifying illustrative trees, recycling, drinking at fountains, checking bike parking/timetables, reading signs and observing lamps. Choosing an action closes the popup; the player first walks to a clear approach and performs its pose. Failed or cancelled approaches do not submit a reward. Tree species describe the original model, not an identification of a surveyed real tree. Climbing or picking can incur an explicitly simulated park fine; fruit actions appear only on the illustrative fruit-tree model.
6. **Save rewards and consequences in the Phone.** **Phone → Wallet → City XP & simulated fines** shows account-backed XP and pending fines in game euros. Migration `014_scenery.sql` stores action receipts and fines. Rewards are limited to one per object/action/day, 200 XP per UTC day, with ten seconds between moments. Retry UUIDs preserve the same receipt. Fine settlement and travel spending serialize on the wallet row; each settled fine has one debit. Wallet balance and ledger responses use one consistent database snapshot, including simultaneous spending/payment. These rules and €2/€3/€5 fines are game design values, not Portuguese law or real payments.

The existing mapped mercados, café terraces and beach umbrellas/visitors remain part of the same geographic world. Close simulation has bounded budgets: up to 26 street vehicles on desktop (18 on narrow views), three trams, 64 street pedestrians (48 on narrow views) and the existing per-beach population budget. Less usable pavement means fewer people. Distant actors pause/hide; three detailed chunks are retained. These limits are not a physical-device frame-rate guarantee.

## Art direction and limits

Original Lisbon-inspired decoration draws on [the Lisbon tourism board's azulejo route](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/lisbon-stories/18-route-du-azulejo), [the Instituto Superior Técnico's Pombaline façade study](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/departamentos/decivil/dissertacao/2353642135026), [Oriente station](https://www.visitlisboa.com/pt-pt/locais/estacao-oriente) and [LX Factory](https://www.visitlisboa.com/pt-pt/locais/lx-fabricas). Their photographs and assets are not copied into ROAM.

Most source buildings have no detailed architectural tags. The extra colours, window forms and roofs are illustrative and not surveyed reconstructions. Roofs remain inside the source footprint and total height. Close ornament is bounded to 64 nearby buildings per chunk within 350 metres; only three geographic chunks are retained. Detail fades by visibility level, without changing mapped street or building positions.

### Street-life placement and limits

Scenic pedestrians use safe sections of actual mapped walking routes within 125 metres of the active location. The current scene requests up to **64 pedestrians on desktop and 48 on narrow/mobile views**, with an independent **72-profile hard cap**. Both clear pavements on a driving street can populate; explicit left/right pavement tags are respected. Wide pedestrian streets use separate walking lanes, and furniture can split a route into multiple safe sections. Building clearance, terrace/park/beach reservations and the source coastline exclude blocked placements. These are maximum budgets; places with fewer safe routes use fewer people.

Seeded uneven spacing, different paces and role-specific pauses avoid repeated ranks of characters. Mercado contexts introduce shoppers with totes, apron-wearing vendors and market staff; cafés introduce local café workers. Visitors and locals can walk in companion pairs. Pairs and three-member families share speed, pauses and direction, retaining their formation through route reversal and large elapsed-time changes. Stroller routes must be non-step pedestrian paths at least 2.4 metres wide, with gentle slopes, additional building clearance and four-metre anchor margins. Child proportions apply only to simulated family members; account characters remain adult models. The scene reuses one position-sample object per walker during animation, and pause integration does not allocate a timing callback each frame. Nearby real players retain separate identity and messaging behavior.

Supplementary street details attach to existing mapped venue façades, using the original POI identity/name. Each chunk allows at most 24 fronts, 24 parasols, 48 planters and 24 menu boards in no more than 13 draw batches. Furniture preserves road widths, a central corridor on wide pedestrian streets, source building/water clearance and terrace reservations. Lantern heights stay below the mounted building's roof. These are original category illustrations rather than verified business exteriors or current menus.

Park paths follow source footways inside source park/wood rings. Each chunk caps path sections at 180, garden benches at 18 and flowerbeds at 24; existing overall landscaping caps remain 420 park trees, 180 roadside trees, 48 benches and 100 lamps. Venue reservations also keep landscaping and walking profiles clear of terraces. Street details regenerate after 120 metres of camera movement; pedestrians recenter after 70 metres. Characters hide and pause in the distant view, and chunk disposal releases instanced buffers. These bounds limit work; they are not physical-device frame-rate measurements.

The latest mercado, denser street population, decorative traffic and beach furnishing changes are described in [lively city scenes](lively-city-scenes.md). Earlier test counts and browser records below remain historical checks of their respective increments.
