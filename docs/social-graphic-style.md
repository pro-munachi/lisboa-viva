# Social graphic style — 9 October 2026

This change applies to the production ROAM app, rather than the standalone onboarding design board. The user’s Nomadtable onboarding recording, five in-app screenshots and colourful game-control screenshot informed the direction: white surfaces, compact app typography, native colour emoji, rounded pill filters, soft cards and a map that remains visible behind the interface. ROAM keeps its own wordmark, cobalt accent and playable 3D Lisbon world.

## Implemented

- `app/social.css` defines shared white surfaces, quiet borders, compact DM Sans headings, cobalt pill actions and pale blue selections. It loads after the existing global and brand styles through the root layout.
- The public welcome page pairs original decorative emoji activity artwork with the real draggable city preview. Its neighbourhood controls still move the camera. Login and registration use the same lighter style, with a compact home link on mobile; their authentication, username rules and submission contracts are unchanged.
- The city dock uses colourful map, luggage, chat and phone emoji on small pastel tiles. The clock uses a sun/cloud or moon based on the simulated trip time. Wallet, nearby-player and radio entry controls also use emoji.
- All **38 existing hotspot and room-action categories** use native colour emoji inside white circular markers. World positions, 44px targets, decluttering, selection, disabled states, keyboard activation and pointer-drag handling remain the existing implementations. A café cup or beach umbrella represents a real mapped place category, rather than a newly invented activity or live customer count.
- The Phone uses ten emoji app tiles, with a compact desktop launcher that exposes every app. Its desktop handset, fixed navigation and full-screen mobile behavior remain. Messages continues to open and operate inside the Phone; draft and retry handling are unchanged.
- Activities uses emoji category pills, white raised cards and a small initial badge derived from the actual API host handle. Counts remain server-provided. There are no fabricated participant portraits, groups or live attendance totals. Room creation, joining, chatting and venue actions retain their existing API contracts.
- Profile uses emoji action tiles. When a browser-saved trip exists, it displays that trip’s actual traveller category and selected interests as soft colour chips. These are read-only summaries; no unsupported preference-editing control was added.
- All **nine venue scene types** use the new interior direction: white and pale-oak floors, cobalt/mint/coral/lilac details, softer furniture, arched accents and sans-serif signage. Main venues have additional lounge seating and tables; transport venues use rounded counters, benches and clear travel graphics. Room backdrops use pale blue or lilac. These remain original imagined interiors, with their existing approximate-layout disclaimer.
- Main public venues now have **four simulated scenic NPCs**, home has **two**, and each transport venue has **three**. Hosts/trainers retain their articulated models; additional main-room visitors merge their coloured body and moving head into two meshes each. Scenic NPCs never count as live players. Their footprints leave furniture, activity arrivals and player placements clear. The existing twelve-player public-room rendering cap is retained; exact public-venue identity and saved appearances still determine real peer rendering.
- Room panels use category emoji circles, distinct corner emoji, rounded progress chips, pastel status cards and cobalt actions. Mobile panels reserve space for the city dock, while the nearby-player row and radio entry move above them. The room card opens compactly with its main action and live status; Room activities expands the corners, music and full venue details. A short imagined-layout note stays visible when collapsed. Existing actions, zone IDs, seat poses, walk routes, boarding targets, music and reduced-motion behavior are retained.
- Journey hubs, modes and recommendations use colourful emoji tiles. Calendar, wardrobe, character editor, nearby players and player menus share the white surface and compact typography. The actual character models and their saved appearance contracts are unchanged.

## Verification

The final emoji source passes the full **347-test web suite**, TypeScript and the production Next.js build. Thirty focused interior/runtime/transit/peer tests passed after the room redesign, including all activity routes and placement of twelve real peers. Added coverage checks exact scenic NPC counts, their clearances, two-mesh cost and paused head motion. Two additional panel tests verify the disclosure targets across all nine types and preserve busy, paused, selected and completed action states. Scene rendering remains inside the existing test budgets: main rooms at most 100 meshes and fewer than 30,000 triangles; transit rooms at most 120 meshes and fewer than 40,000 triangles. These are scene-census limits, not measured frame rates. The received main update passed **209 Go tests including subtests**, with the race detector and real PostgreSQL, with no failures or skips; see [the main update note](main-pull-2026-10-09.md).

The local preview runs with the newly built API on port 8082, which applied the additive main migrations. In the actual browser, the desktop Phone showed all ten emoji apps, and Messages opened inside it. A 390 × 844 viewport showed a full-screen mobile Messages inbox with fixed Home navigation. The Activities screen rendered its emoji filters and honest empty state; populated activity cards and participant initials were not separately browser-verified in this review. The rebuilt gym showed its new palette, rounded seating, four scenic locals, native emoji action markers and the white room panel. Café Janis was also opened in the final build. At 390 × 844, its collapsed card left the room visible; Room activities expanded all corner controls and venue details. At 390 × 844 and 844 × 390, DOM hit tests confirmed that Phone, Find nearby players and Exit were reachable, without page-width overflow. The viewport override was reset after review. Browser observation is distinct from the automated behavior checks; no message, booking or purchase was submitted during the visual review.
